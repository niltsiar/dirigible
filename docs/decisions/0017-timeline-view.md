## Context

Release 1 run 4b (`docs/requirements/releases.md`) adds the track lanes with clips and waveforms, the playhead line, the markers ruler, zoom, a scroll that follows the playhead, and the selection that run 4c uses. A 2 h, 6-track session must draw at 60 fps while it plays. The main actor never reads a WAV, and a take never waits for peaks. Hindenburg research: `docs/research/timeline-view.md`.

Probes on 2026-09-25 (MacBook Pro 2018, Intel i5-8259U, Iris Plus 655, macOS 15.7.9, Swift 6.2.4):

- P1. A 2 h mono 24-bit WAV (1.04 GB) gives peaks of 256-frame bins in 0.39 s with a warm cache. A cold SSD read of the file takes 0.35 s. The read block must be a multiple of the bin.
- F1 to F6. On APFS, the size and the mtime in ns change at a same-length rewrite. A copy (`ditto`, `FileManager.copyItem`) and a move keep them. A copy changes the inode. On exFAT, the mtime step is 10 ms, and a same-length rewrite within 10 ms keeps the stamp.
- D1 to D6. At 2880 x 1200 px with 6 lanes: Core Graphics fills of waveform columns take 160 to 1221 ms per full draw. Own pixels into a reused buffer take 4.0 to 5.3 ms p95. A full redraw at each frame gives 20 fps (AppKit) and 30 fps (SwiftUI Canvas). A scroll view whose document view is as wide as the session and draws only its dirty rect, with a `CALayer` playhead, gives 60 fps with page follow and with continuous follow, also at 10.8 million pt wide.

## Decision

**Drawing.** The lanes are an `NSScrollView` in the SwiftUI window. Its document view is flipped and layer-backed, as wide as the session at the zoom, and draws only its dirty rect. It writes BGRA pixels into a buffer (a background pattern, then column spans for the peak and the RMS), then draws one image over the buffer, with no copy. The context of the draw is a display list (macOS 15.7.9): it draws the image later, and again after a scroll. So the data provider of the image holds the buffer, and a draw uses a buffer again only when no image holds it. A reused buffer showed the pixels of a later draw after a page turn, and a buffer of another size was freed under its image. The layer keeps the image of each region until a later draw covers it or the region needs display again. A scroll marks the lanes more than one view away in a later job of the main queue: no draw, and their images go (4 live images or less after each of 8 page turns). A mark next to the view made the layer draw all lanes in view. A partial draw (an edit or a drag event) also draws the partial draws before it, so it covers the image of the last one: a clip drag kept one image per event. When that union is more than a quarter of the lanes in view larger than the new rect, one full draw frees every image (4 live images or less over 60 drag events). The playhead is a `CALayer` that the `NSView.displayLink` tick moves; the tick reads the playhead outside SwiftUI. The ruler is an `NSRulerView`. The track headers are SwiftUI rows in the vertical ruler of the scroll view, and they share the lane height. Lanes that do not fit the window scroll vertically. The top of the clip view places the headers, so each header stays beside its lane, and a wheel over the headers scrolls the lanes. The clip view goes under both rulers, so the lanes in view are the clip bounds minus its content insets. A page turn and a zoom keep the vertical origin. The document view and the ruler each keep an `Equatable` value of their drawn inputs (the session, the peaks, the missing media, the selection, the zoom, the lane height). They redraw only when that value changes or the backing scale changes. The playhead is not a drawn input. `Studio.missingMedia` is a stored property of its own, not a field of the playback state, so the 33 ms playhead poll does not update the view.

**Waveform.** Rectified, -R dB to 0 dB, R one of the nine ranges of `WaveformRange` (24, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84, 96, 120, and 145 dB; the range of the window, the app default at its open), light peaks over dark RMS, with clip gain plus track gain and the fades of 0035 (of the sounding clip, 0036). A stereo track draws the maximum of both channels. A missing or short clip draws red. The red comes only from `Studio.missingMedia`.

**Peaks.** Three levels of 256, 4096, and 65536 frames per bin, a UInt32 peak and a UInt32 RMS per bin, a linear magnitude with `UInt32.max` = 0 dBFS. The lowest step is -192.7 dBFS, so a 24-bit signal keeps its level down to its last bit and draws at 145 dB. `Studio` computes them off the main actor, one file at a time, and pauses before each block of 262,144 frames while a take starts, records, or finalizes. After a pause, the file starts again with a new `stat`. The pass opens each WAV with `openMedia`, as the Player and the media check do, and merges its faults into `missingMedia`. `openMedia` also gives the stamp of the descriptor that it opened. A read error gives `.unreadable` in `missingMedia`. Studio starts a pass at the open, after a finalize, and after an edit, an undo, or a redo that changes the pool files. The window starts one at focus, because a WAV can change outside the app. The views read only `Studio.peaks`.

**Close.** `settle()` first sets a flag: from then, a request for a pass does nothing, and a running pass stops at its next file, its next block, or its next 100 ms wait. `settle()` ends the take, then cancels the pass and waits for it, then clears the flag. The last two steps run on every exit of `settle()`. So settle always ends a take within a bound, and no task holds the package lock after a close. A cancelled close or a cancelled Quit asks for a pass again.

**Cache.** One file per pool WAV in `Peaks/` in the folder of the bundle ID of the app in `~/Library/Caches/` (the command-line front end: `~/Library/Caches/com.delineas.dirigible/Peaks/`), named by the pool path, the size, and the mtime of the WAV, then `.v2.peaks`. The format is version 2: a 64-byte little-endian header with the magic `DIRPEAKS` and the bytes of a bin (8), then the bins. A version 1 file (UInt16 values, a name that ends in the mtime and `.peaks`) is a miss, and it stays until the prune. The reader refuses a file whose fields do not match the WAV, and a refusal is a miss. A newer version is a miss (0024 P5). A write error keeps the peaks in memory. The package does not change, so 0003 does not change. A cache hit of a pass sets the modification time of the cache file to now. At launch, the app removes each file in the cache folder that has a modification time older than 90 days, off the main actor. A write removes no other cache file: pool paths repeat in each package (`take1-1-1.wav`), so a cache file of the same pool path with another stamp can be the peaks of another package.

**Selection and view state.** The in point, the out point, and the selected tracks, the zoom, and Automatic Scroll are window state. None is saved. The selection has one read: a range and at least one track that exists now, or nothing.

**Record-over warning.** The record-over chip over the track headers shows only when Record can reach a clip on an armed track that is not in view. Record replaces from the playhead on, and the take length is not known at the start. Thus the rule reads only the first clip of each armed track that ends after the playhead. When that clip is in view, a later clip of the track that is not in view gets no warning. The first clip is not in view when less than half of its lane is in the visible lanes (above them, under them, or under the time ruler), or when the first frame of the clip that Record reaches (the playhead or the clip start, the later one) is outside the visible frames. Before the first layout, nothing counts as in view. A window minimum height for all lanes was rejected, because the brief sets no track limit.

**Joins and playhead.** A join is 2 pt wide and as high as its lane, centered on the cut between two clips that touch, on every track, when both boxes are `LaneGeometry.grabWidth` (12 pt) or wider. It is the lane color in the dark palette and 0x5A7BA6 in the light palette. The lane renderer draws it after the waveforms, the crossfade lines, and the gain line, so they do not cover it. The live take, the range band, the clip-set tint, and the marker lines draw over it. A draw of some columns draws the join of a cut in or at the edge of its columns, so a trim of the left clip redraws it. The playhead line is a 1 pt core with a 1 pt halo on each side. The core is white in dark mode and 0x1D1D1F in light mode, and 2 pt wide with Increase Contrast. The halo is black at 70 % or white at 85 %. During a take the same layer is the record head and the core is systemRed. The line follows an appearance change with no tick. The overview strip playhead stays red. The head on the ruler is a pentagon of 11 x 10 pt (13 x 12 pt with Increase Contrast) in the top 10 pt of the ruler, in the color of the line with the halo as outline. It is a layer in a container that starts at the left edge of the lanes in view, so no part of it shows over the track headers. A play tick, a scroll, a zoom, and a change of the header width move it by position and draw no ruler pixel. When the head is near a time label, it covers part of the label.

**Follow.** Automatic Scroll turns pages as in Hindenburg: at 94% of the width the view moves so the playhead is at 11%. A user scroll that moves the view horizontally during play or a take holds the follow until the head leaves the view and is in view again, a seek, a play start, or a take start. A hold that ended at the first tick with the head in view let the follow undo a trackpad scroll back (live take view, Phase 7 V3). A scroll with no horizontal move (a vertical scroll, a mouse brush, a scroller click) holds nothing: its hold never ended, because the head did not leave the view (follow-hold fix). During a live scroll (a scroller knob drag, a trackpad gesture), the follow turns no page; at its end, the hold starts only when the view moved. During a take, the follow reads the record head, and the timeline end grows a minute at a time so each turn has room (live take view). The end of a take is not a seek: the view stays.

**Keys.** View: Zoom In (Command-plus), Zoom Out (Command-minus), Zoom to Session (Command-0), Zoom to Selection (H), Automatic Scroll (Option-Command-F). Edit: Set In Point (I), Set Out Point (O), Deselect All (Shift-Command-A), Select All (the system item). Esc empties the selection through `cancelOperation(_:)`; it has no menu item, because a menu item would take Esc from alerts and sheets.

**CLI (0011).** The peak compute reads the audio files of a package, so the 0011 clause for a feature that operates on an audio file applies. `dirigible peaks <package>` reads the cache and writes nothing: for each pool file, `hit` with the bin count, `miss`, or `missing`. It reads the cache only through the public function `peakCacheStates(of:package:cache:)`. It takes no lock and starts no compute. The app uses the folder of its own bundle ID, so a verify copy with another ID has its own cache. With no `--cache`, the CLI reads the folder of the real app (`com.delineas.dirigible`). `--cache <folder>` changes it.

## Consequences

- 0002 gets an amended line: `DirigibleApp/Timeline/` holds the timeline view, and `Studio+Peaks.swift` loads the peaks.
- 0016 gets an amended line: the record-over warning of the transport bar shows only for a clip that is not in view, because the lanes draw the clips. History: 0023 replaced the transport bar with the overlay, and the rule changed on 2026-09-29 (see the header).
- 0011 gets an amended line: `dirigible peaks` reads the peak cache.
- A package on another Mac, or after a cache purge, computes its peaks again: under 1 s per 2 h track on an SSD, about 10 s on a USB hard disk (inference).
- Closer than 256 frames per pixel, the waveform is blocky. The zoom stops at 128 frames per point.
- An exFAT rewrite of a WAV with the same length within 10 ms keeps a stale cache hit. No in-app path rewrites a WAV that fast.
- Orphan cache files stay after a WAV rewrite or a package delete for 90 days after their last use, or until the system purges Caches.
- The pass does not pause during play. A manual check on a USB hard disk decides it; if play drops out, the pass also pauses during play.

## Alternatives considered

- **`Peaks/` in the package.** The peaks move to another Mac with the package. Rejected: a lock-holder write path, a read-only-package case, and a 0003 change, for data that computes in under 1 s per 2 h track.
- **Remove the record-over warning.** Rejected: a lane under the visible lanes, or a playhead off screen, hides a clip on an armed track.
- **Warn also for a clip in view.** Rejected on 2026-09-29: the lanes draw the clip. With this rule, the warning shows in almost every edited session until the playhead is at the end.
- **Peaks in memory only.** Rejected: a reopen of a 6-track 2 h session on a slow disk computes for about 60 s each time.
- **SwiftUI `Canvas`, or a full redraw at each frame.** Rejected: 30 fps and 20 fps (D3, D4).
- **Core Graphics path or rect fills.** Rejected: 160 to 1221 ms per full draw (D1).
- **A centered, fixed playhead.** Rejected for now: Hindenburg PRO 2 turns pages. The probe shows that it would also reach 60 fps.
- **An Esc menu item.** Rejected: it takes the key from alerts and sheets.
