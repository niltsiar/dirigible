## Context

Release 1 run 4d (`docs/requirements/releases.md`) adds the mouse edits on the lanes and the ruler: a click that selects a clip, a drag that moves it (also to a lane of the same layout), an edge drag that trims it, Shift-drag, the drag of a marker flag and of the in and out triangles, the ruler menu, and the cursors (F13). It adds no Core edit and no `dirigible` command (0018). Hindenburg research: `docs/research/mouse-edits.md`.
Constraints:

- `TimelineScrollView.update(_:)` sets the lane inputs at each SwiftUI update (peaks, the media check, the Marks). A preview in those inputs is lost in the middle of a drag.
- `SessionWindow.edit` shows an alert on each throw. A `move` replayed after an undo throws `.unknownClip`.
- `Marks` and `TimelineScale` are pure, but they are in the executable target `DirigibleApp`. CODING_STANDARDS.md asks for a thin app target and logic in library targets.
- At 2 h, 6 tracks, and 2,000 clips per track, a `move` on a copy takes 3.6 ms p95 and a lane draw of 2880 x 1200 px 4.6 ms p95 (optimized, 0018 probe P4, 0017).

Probes on 2026-09-26 (MacBook Pro 2018, Intel i5-8259U, macOS 15.7.9, Swift 6.2.4):

- C1. `NSCursor` arrow, iBeam, openHand, closedHand, pointingHand, and resizeLeftRight compile with `-warnings-as-errors` at the macOS 14 target. `columnResize` is macOS 15 and later.
- L1. The clip labels at fit zoom (12,000 clips in 1440 pt) take 330 ms p95 per draw. With no label in a box under 30 pt, 0 ms. 216 labels take 5.7 ms p95.

## Decision

**Module.** A new library target `DirigibleTimeline` depends on `DirigibleCore` and imports Foundation only. It holds `Marks` and `TimelineScale` (moved from `DirigibleApp`), the lane and ruler geometry, `trimLimits`, the cursor kinds, and `TimelineGesture`. `DirigibleApp` depends on it. The views forward mouse events and map cursor kinds to `NSCursor`. `LaneGeometry` and `RulerGeometry` are the one geometry: the views and `LaneRenderer` draw with them and the hit test reads them, so the drawn box is the hit box. `changedFrames` gives the frames and the lanes where two sessions, or the frames where two Marks, draw differently; the document view redraws only those columns, and `LaneRenderer` can draw a range of lanes. `trimLimits` and the hit types are internal; their tests use `@testable import DirigibleTimeline`. `ClipLane.index(holding:)` becomes public; it is a binary search and changes nothing.

**One gesture model.** `TimelineGesture` is a value type with one state enum, `TimelineGesture.State`. `handle(_:_:)` takes a typed event (down, drag, up, escape, sessionChanged) and a context (the session, the Marks, the playhead, the geometry, `canEdit`), and returns typed effects (Marks, seek, current marker, commit). A point has a tagged space (`Place.lanes`, `Place.ruler`); only `LaneGeometry` converts points to frames. No public API returns a lane index.

**Preview.** The scroll view owns the gesture and keeps the last SwiftUI inputs as its source. It draws the preview over the source at each update, so a SwiftUI update does not remove it. The preview never enters observable window state. At a mouse-down, the window commits the typed track names first, and the source takes the Studio session, so a rename just before a drag does not drop the drag. A drag event does not read the Studio session: a change during the drag reaches the gesture through the next SwiftUI update, which drops it. At the mouse-up, the window applies the effects first, then the source takes the Studio session, then the view draws, so the lanes never draw the old session. A preview edit that throws keeps the last preview; no gesture input reaches that case.

**Commit.** At the mouse-up, the gesture returns a `GestureCommit` with the session of the mouse-down (`base`) and the result of one Core edit on it (`move`, `trim`, or `setMarker`). `apply(to:)` sets the session to the result only when the session is still `base`. It never throws. So an undo, a redo, or another edit during a drag drops the gesture with no alert and no undo entry, and a take drops it through the 0018 idle gate. A drag is one undo entry.

**Rules.** The zones of a clip box: 4 pt inside each edge trims, the upper two thirds moves, the lower third and a gap make a range (0017). A box under 12 pt is all range, so a small drag at fit zoom cannot move a clip that the user cannot see. Shift or Command at the mouse-down makes a range press. A drag of a Shift press with no Command draws a marquee (0038). A drag starts after 3 pt of movement. The time of a move, a trim, a flag, or a triangle changes only after 3 pt of horizontal movement, so the jitter of a click edits nothing. A drag back to its start is a click. A move goes to another lane after half a lane of vertical movement, when the layout of that lane matches, else it stays on its lane; it overwrites the clips under it (0003); its start is 0 or more. A trim stops at a neighbor, at the file, and at a length of 1 pt at the zoom (at least 1 frame). On the ruler, a triangle comes before a flag, and a flag before the time area. A press in the time area seeks at the down. A drag of 3 pt or more there makes a range from the press frame on the header tracks, or on all tracks when none is selected, and the up seeks to the in point. A flag takes x from its position - 3 pt to its position + 6 pt (the line and its box). A ruler mouse-down makes the lanes the first responder, so Esc reaches the gesture and not a focused text field. A triangle stops before the other point. The ruler menu adds a marker or a chapter at the clicked frame and deletes the flag under the pointer. Esc during a gesture cancels only the gesture. A clip label draws only in a box of 60 pt or more (L1).

**Budget.** One drag event (the gesture step, the preview edit, the SwiftUI update of new Marks, and the draw with labels at 2880 x 1200 px) takes 16 ms p95 or less in an optimized build, with one event per 60 Hz frame: `make bench FILTER=MouseEventBenchmark`. The measured paths, each at fit zoom and at the zoom of 60 pt boxes: a move of a clip narrower than the view, an end-edge trim, a flag drag, a range drag, an in-triangle drag, a move of the last clip past the session end, a move of a clip wider than the view, and a move of a wide clip to the next lane. The full draw of the lanes in view is a separate case.

**CLI (0011).** No command. The gesture changes window state and calls `move`, `trim`, `setMarker`, and `removeMarker`, which have `dirigible clip move`, `clip trim`, `marker set`, and `marker remove`.

## Consequences

- 0002 gets an amended line: `DirigibleTimeline` (library) holds the timeline selection and the gesture model; `DirigibleApp` depends on it.
- 0017 gets an amended line: a click on a clip body selects the clip and does not seek; a narrow clip has no label.
- `MarksTests` moves to `DirigibleTimelineTests`. Test files that use `Marks` import `DirigibleTimeline`.
- `ImportRuleTests` checks that `DirigibleTimeline` imports only Foundation and DirigibleCore.
- A drag event redraws only the columns that changed: the old and new boxes of the clips that a move or a trim changes (also the clips that a move cuts), the old and new line of a moved marker, and the lines and band part of the changed Marks points. A rect of 360 pt or more of clip changes takes only the rows of the changed lanes, so a move of a clip wider than the view redraws one or two lanes (17.7 ms p95 at full height, 4.3 ms with the rows, 60 pt boxes). A narrower rect, a marker, and the Marks take the full height: a narrow rect of part of the height made the flush wait for the window server (11 ms p95 against 3 ms on the Intel Mac). The 360 pt is a calibration value for manual check M8. A change of another input (the tracks, their gain or layout, the files, the peaks, the zoom, the lane height) redraws the lanes in view.
- During a gesture the lanes keep their width, and the mouse-up gives the new width. AppKit draws the new columns of a wider view at full height and joins them to the rect of the event. The part past the lanes is not in view, because the lanes are at least as wide as the view. A new width redraws the lanes in view: with the redraw policy `onSetNeedsDisplay`, the screen showed the old pixels scaled to the new width.
- Invariant: `changedFrames` compares every Session value that the lanes draw. A new drawn value needs a compare there, else a drag event leaves an old drawing on the screen.

## Alternatives considered

- **The gesture in the app target, the preview in the document view.** Rejected: the gesture tests need a window and a studio, and the app target grows.
- **The preview as observable window state.** Rejected: each drag event would update SwiftUI, and the drawn session would have two owners.
- **Replay the edit at the mouse-up and catch the throw.** Rejected: it covers only the listed errors; an edit that does not throw on a changed session would commit onto the wrong state.
- **One type per gesture behind a protocol.** Rejected: one conformer each (CODING_STANDARDS.md).
