- Amended: 2026-10-15, run `2026-10-15-export-loudness`. ## Context

0008 asks for export normalization: measure the mix, apply one static gain, limit the true peak to -1 dBTP, measure again, and show the result in the export report. Its presets are -16 (podcast), -18 (speech), -23 (EBU R 128), and -24 (US broadcast) LUFS, each 3 LU lower for a mono export. 0019 writes one pass, rounds 16-bit with no dither (P3), and keeps each export repeatable and FLAC equal to WAV bit for bit. The constraints:

- Apple's `AUPeakLimiter` has no ceiling parameter and is probably a sample-peak limiter: an fs/4 sine at 45 degrees reads 3 dB over its sample peak. A limiter on it needs a lower ceiling, a check, and a retry.
- AES TD1004 puts -1 dBTP at the input of a lossy codec.
- A modal alert takes the Record key (`MenuKeys.swift`, `UndoRouting.swift`).

Probes of the run (`.scratchpads/runs/2026-10-03-export-loudness/probe/`): P1 reads Tech 3341 cases 15 to 19 within tolerance at 44.1, 48, and 96 kHz with the 4x FIR of BS.1770-5 Annex 2. L1 and the candidate A probes hold a look-ahead limiter on that detector at most 0.0013 dB over its ceiling on 22 signals; the Swift limiter reads at most 0.000002 dB over its internal ceiling on 12 of them. D1 shows that TPDF dither of ±1 LSB keeps a 0.25 LSB level (mean 0.2513 LSB) where the rounding gives 0.

## Decision

**Meter.** `TruePeakFilter` in `DirigibleAudio/DSP/TruePeak.swift` runs the 4 phases of 12 taps of Annex 2 with `vDSP_conv`, with an 11-frame history per channel. Per frame it gives the largest of |sample| and |each phase output| of the window that ends there. 4x at every rate. `TruePeakMeter` keeps the maximum; digital silence gives nil.

**Limiter.** `TruePeakLimiter` in `DSP/Limiter.swift` is our own, with one gain for all channels. Per frame: the needed gain `min(1, c / truePeak)` with an internal ceiling c of -1.05 dBTP; the minimum over 5 ms plus the 12 FIR frames; a one-pole release of 50 ms; and the mean of the last 5 ms. The audio is delayed by 5 ms plus 10 frames. A drain at the end gives back the delay, so the output length equals the input length for any chunk size. Under the ceiling the gain is exactly 1, so the output equals the input bit for bit.

**Passes.** With a target, `exportMix` renders the source at least twice. Pass 1 measures the mix (the mono fold for a mono export) and its true peak before the temporary file exists. The first gain is `target - measured` with no limit. When the true peak of the mix plus the gain is over the ceiling minus `TruePeakLimiter.margin` (0.05 dB), the limiter acts and the output reads lower than the target. Then trial passes run before the write: each renders the mix with a gain and the limiter and measures the output, with no file. The next gain adds `target - output`, and then the secant of the last two points. A round of trial passes stops within 0.1 LU of the target or after 4 trial passes, and keeps the gain with the smallest miss. The write pass applies that gain, the limiter, and the dither. A mix with no gated block gets 0 dB and no trial pass. `LoudnessSearch` in `Render/ExportLoudness.swift` decides each step. The progress maps pass 1 to 0...0.15, the trial passes of the first round to 0.15...0.3, and the write to 0.3...0.75 (0.15...0.75 with no trial pass, and up to 1 with no read-back). The read-back and each retry round share 0.75...1 in equal bands, so a retry round moves the bar in proportion to its work. At most 101 calls. A cancel in any pass leaves no file. `ExportOptions.loudness` is nil by default, and `ExportOptions.render` keeps nil, so `dirigible render` and an export with Off do not change. `exportMix` and `Studio.export` return `ExportResult`: the faults and the `LoudnessReport` (target, measured, gain, output loudness, true peak). The gain is the one that the file got.

**Read-back.** A file that the encoder or the resampler changes is read back: an AAC or MP3 file, and a WAV, FLAC, or float CAF file at a file rate other than the session rate. An export with Off has no search and no read-back. After the write `exportMix` decodes the temporary file as macOS decodes it (AudioToolbox) and measures its loudness and true peak with the same meters. The decode of a 2 h file takes about 18 s (AAC) and 24 s (MP3), plus the meters (P3). A file that Core Audio cannot open or read, and a decode with fewer frames than the written signal has at the file rate (less one frame), throws `.writeFailed`. A read that fails after those frames, on the padding of the last MP3 packet, ends the file. The search starts with a ceiling 1.5 dB under -1 dBTP for AAC and 0.5 dB for MP3, and with the LAME offset of 0034 for MP3 (probes U10, U19). A read-back replaces the codec offset with the measured change: the effective target is the target plus the loudness of the limited signal minus the loudness of the file. A new round runs when the decoded true peak is over -1.0 dBTP, with the ceiling set the excess plus 0.1 dB under the lower of the round ceiling and the true peak of the signal that the encoder got (a limiter that did nothing leaves the round ceiling above that peak, and the same encode would repeat), or when the decoded loudness is more than 0.2 LU off the target and the trial cap did not stop the last round. The 0.1 LU tolerance holds for the limited signal before the encoder. The decoded file is accepted within 0.2 LU of the target and at or under -1.0 dBTP. At most 2 retry rounds run, and a round stops its trial passes after 4. When a cap stops the search, the last file stays, it can be short of the target, and the report shows its read-back values. The report is the read-back where it ran. The tag copy of an M4A comes after the read-back.

**Dither.** `TPDFDither` in `DSP/Dither.swift` adds ±1 LSB of 16 bit to WAV 16 and FLAC 16, with and without a target: the difference of two uniform values from SplitMix64, one stream per channel, with a fixed seed that starts again at each export. So each export of the same mix gives the same file, and FLAC equals WAV bit for bit. 24-bit, AAC, MP3, and the float CAF get no dither.

**App.** The save panel has a Loudness pop-up: Off, Podcast, Speech, EBU R 128, and US broadcast, each with its LUFS value for the chosen channels. The default is Podcast. `export.loudness` stores a token (`off` or the preset), so Off comes back as Off; a missing or unknown value gives Podcast. A clean export with a target shows the report and the file name in a non-modal info banner (`WindowBanner.exportReport`, [0023](0023-ui-shell.md)), which VoiceOver reads. The banner shows only while the session revision (`History.revision`) equals the revision at the export start: the next export, take, edit, undo, or redo removes it, and an edit during the export keeps it from showing. A gain over the ±20 dB limit of auto-level makes it a warning with a hint to check the selection; the gain has no limit. Record works while it shows. With a file that rendered as silence, the one alert of 0019 names the files and has the report line.

**CLI.** `dirigible export ... [--loudness podcast|speech|ebu|atsc]` prints one line with the values of the file: `loudness -16.0 LUFS, true peak -1.2 dBTP, gain +3.4 dB`, with `none` for no measurement. An unknown name exits 64. With no `--loudness`, the output does not change.

## Consequences

- An export with a target renders the mix twice when the limiter changes no frame, and 1 + k + 1 times when it acts, with k trial passes (4 at most). The 2 h, 8-track bench to WAV 16 with Podcast takes 37 s with no limiting (the budget is 57 s) and 94 s with hard limiting and 4 trial passes (the budget is 120 s) (`make bench FILTER='LoudnessExportBenchmark PeakyLoudnessExportBenchmark'`).
- A 16-bit export with Off changes too: its last bit has dither, so it differs from an export of an earlier version.
- An export with Off still clips a mix over full scale.
- An export at a file rate other than the session rate is limited at the session rate with a ceiling 0.3 dB lower, the worst rise of the resampler, and then read back at the file rate. The converter from 48 to 44.1 kHz added 0.00 dB on two real masters (probe P10) and 0.09 dB on the train fixture. The resampler also filters the dither a little.
- The report is the written file as macOS decodes it, where the read-back runs. The 2 h, 8-track bench to AAC 128 with Podcast takes 115 s with no limiting (one read-back) and 181 s with hard limiting, 4 trial passes, one encode, and one read-back. The budgets are 147 s and 240 s, and 420 s with one retry (`make bench FILTER='anAACPodcastExportOfTheSineFixtureFitsTheBudget anAACPodcastExportOfTheTrainFixtureFitsTheBudget'`).
- The search makes up the limiter loss: the gain rises until the limited output is within 0.1 LU of the target, and the report shows the gain that the file got.
- Each Export Selection is measured and normalized by itself.

## Alternatives considered

- **`AUPeakLimiter` with a trim and a retry (candidate B).** Rejected: a black box with no ceiling parameter, up to 3 passes, and 9 probes before code; its repeatability is unknown.
- **A feedback limiter with no look-ahead.** Rejected: it cannot catch the first peak.
- **`kAudioConverterPropertyDithering`.** Rejected for now: its repeatability and its FLAC path are not probed, and a random dither breaks both properties.
- **A gain limit of ±20 dB, as auto-level.** Rejected: a limit misses the target with no reason that the user sees; the report shows the gain.
- **The report in a modal alert.** Rejected: it takes the Record key.
