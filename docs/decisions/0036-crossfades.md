## Context

Release 2 run 11 asks for crossfades (F33). Decision 0035 planned them: an equal-power crossfade at a butt join, centered on the join, with no overlap of clips, from the media handles, in `session.xml` version 4. It left two questions open: a handle that is too short, and a crossfade at a join that an edit breaks. Hindenburg makes a crossfade from a selection with Command-F and changes its length with a handle drag. Logic Pro pivots a crossfade on the region junction. Final Cut Pro offers an overlap (a ripple edit) when the handles are short. Probe X1 measured the Float ramp of `sineRamp`: |a^2 + b^2 - 1| <= 2.2e-7 for L = 1 to 96,001 frames. The run splits in two parts: 11c-1 (the model, the format, the CLI, and the render) and 11c-2 (Command-F at a join, the handle drag, the draw, and the benches).

## Decision

Notation: clip A ends at the join J, and clip B starts at J. L is the crossfade length.

- **Fields.** A clip has `crossfades: Crossfades`, apart from `fades`, with a `start` and an `end` length in frames. `Crossfades.init?` refuses an odd length, a negative length, and a length over `Frames.limit`. 0 is no crossfade. At a join, A.crossfades.end equals B.crossfades.start, so each clip holds its own half. `Crossfades` and `Fades` hold no curve: the curve of an edge follows from the join kind, so `ClipLane` gives it (**Sounding clip**). The render and the draw read the sounding clips from the lane, and only the lane reads the neighbor. The 11b-1 handle and the 11b-2 inspector rebuild `Fades`, so a separate field keeps them from dropping a crossfade.
- **Sounding clip.** `ClipLane.sounding(at:)` gives a `SoundingClip` for the clip at an index. Its `clip` is the clip as it sounds: L/2 frames of media handle more at each edge with a crossfade, and the crossfade of that edge as the fade of that edge. Its `curves` (`Fades.Curves`, with `start` and `end`) give the curve of the join at each edge. A butt join gives the sine curve of 0035 (equal power): a(i) = sin(pi/2 * (L - i) / L) and b(i) = sin(pi/2 * i / L). A seamless join gives the linear curve (equal gain): a(i) = (L - i) / L and b(i) = i / L. An edge with no crossfade gives the sine curve, because a fade is always sine. `ClipLane.sounding(in:)` gives the sounding clips whose sounding range has a frame in a range, for the render. `ClipLane.clips(soundingIn:)` stays: the media check and `changedFrames` read only the stored clips and their frames. `Clip.soundingRange` is public, for a reader that needs only the frames (`changedFrames`). No public `Clip.sounding` is left, so no reader can get the sine curve at a seamless join. `Fades` holds no curve, so a stored `Clip.fades` always plays the sine curve. `Fades.gain(at:length:curves:)` and the `gainFactors` of `MixReader` take the curves as a parameter with no default value. A reader of a stored clip with no crossfade passes `Fades.Curves.sine`; a reader of a sounding clip passes its `curves`. `gainFactors` writes the linear ramp with `vDSP_vramp` and no `vvsinf`.
- **Format.** `<clip ... crossfadeOut="960"/>` on A and `<clip ... crossfadeIn="960"/>` on B. The writer writes each attribute only when it is not 0, and a missing one or a written 0 reads as 0. `Session.fileVersion` is 4 when a clip has a crossfade, else the 0035 rule. From run `2026-10-17-agent-pack`, it is 9 when a clip has a crossfade at a seamless join, else the rule of 0055 and before. A gain envelope point gives 10 and a track role 11 (0060), and `sessionVersion` is 11. No attribute is new: the curve follows from the join kind. An internal `ClipLane` query gives the left clip of the first seamless join with a crossfade; `fileVersion` and the reader check use it. The reader refuses:
  - a crossfade attribute in a file of version 3 or lower, and an odd, negative, or too long value, with `.invalidValue`;
  - a crossfade with no touching partner, or two partners of different lengths, with `.crossfadeWithoutJoin`;
  - a crossfade at a seamless join in a file of version 8 or lower, with `.crossfadeAtSeamlessJoin` for the left clip. `SessionReader` holds this check after it builds each lane, because only the reader knows the file version. A file of version 9 with a crossfade at a seamless join reads, also with equal gains;
  - a fade and a crossfade at one edge with `.fadeAtCrossfade`;
  - a short media handle with `.crossfadeOutsideFile(clip, needed: L/2)`.
- **Media-handle limit.** `Session.check` refuses a clip with less than L/2 frames of file before its start crossfade (L/2 <= offset) or after its end crossfade (offset + length + L/2 <= file length), with `.crossfadeOutsideFile(clip, needed: L/2)`. It refuses a fade at an edge with a crossfade with `.fadeAtCrossfade`. There is no overlap and no ripple: `MixReader` cuts a short read with no error, which is an audible dip. `crossfadeLimit(after: index, in: lane)` is 2 x min(handle after A, handle before B, room of A, room of B), rounded down to even. A seamless join has the same limit: the crossfade reads the frames of the file on each side of J there too. The limit is 0 where a new crossfade changes no sample (**Seamless join**). It is nil when no clip touches the end of A or the file of A is not in the pool. Command-F and the handle drag of 11c-2 clamp to it.
- **Room.** The reach of an edge is its fade plus half its crossfade. `Clip.room(edge)` is the length minus the reach of the other edge, with no special case. `Session.check` refuses reach(start) + reach(end) over the length with `.fadeOutsideClip`. The fade handle, the inspector, and `Clip.fades(setting:to:)` clamp a fade to the room of its edge, and give no fade at an edge with a crossfade. A fade yields to a crossfade: `Clip.fitting` fits the fades into the length minus both crossfade halves. In `crossfadeLimit`, the room counts the crossfade half at the other edge and not its fade, because that fade yields.
- **Set.** `Session.setCrossfades([ClipID: Frames])` takes the left clip of each join. L > 0 sets the crossfade on both clips, sets the fades at the two join edges to 0, and makes the fade at the other edge of each clip yield. L = 0 removes the crossfade from both clips and leaves the fades. A clip with no touching right neighbor throws `.crossfadeWithoutJoin`, also for L = 0. L > 0 throws `.crossfadeAtEqualGains` where a new crossfade changes no sample (**Seamless join**). A kept crossfade there can change its length or go to 0. A length that `Crossfades` refuses throws `.frameOutOfRange`, and an unknown clip `.unknownClip`. It checks each changed clip, then writes all the changed clips of a lane before the repair reads their joins, so a crossfade of two clips is never half written. One pass and one undo entry; a throw changes nothing.
- **Drop at a broken join.** An edit that breaks a join removes the crossfade from both clips. `ClipLane` keeps a crossfade only when its neighbor touches the edge and both lengths are equal. A join that changes kind and keeps its crossfade (a butt join that becomes seamless, or the reverse) changes no stored value: the sounding clip reads the new curve. The kind changes only when one of the two clips changes, and the sounding range of each clip covers J - L/2 to J + L/2, so `changedFrames` needs no new rule. `clear`, `split`, `overwrite`, and `remove(ids:)` end with a repair of the whole lane, because they cut or remove clips that can leave an orphan on a clip that the edit did not touch. `insert` repairs the joins next to each inserted clip, and `remove(at:)` repairs the join of its two new neighbors. This is complete because every mutator leaves the lane paired and every clip has a positive length. `replace(at: i)` repairs only the joins of clips i - 1 to i + 1: one clip changes and no clip moves, so `setFades`, `setGains`, and `setCrossfades` stay linear. A cut part (split, clear, overwrite, excerpt) keeps the crossfade of an edge of the clip that it keeps only when its half is not more than the room of that edge; the repair then drops the partner. A trim keeps the crossfade of each edge that does not move, and the fades yield to it. A paste, a move, and a nudge keep the crossfades; the repair drops each one whose join does not come with it. A move to the same track and start, and a nudge by 0, change nothing. `CrossfadeTests.randomEditsKeepTheSessionSavable` runs 500 seeded edit sequences, and each saved session reads back.
- **Seamless join.** A join is seamless when B is in the file of A at offset A.offset + A.length. There the handles of A and B hold one signal s, and equal power makes it 3 dB too loud at J. The linear curve gives a(i) + b(i) = 1 (probe P4 of run `2026-10-17-agent-pack`: |a + b - 1| <= 1.2e-7 in Float for L = 2 to 96,000). So the sum is s x (gA x a + gB x b): a gain ramp from gA to gB that never passes the larger gain. A fade-out of A or a fade-in of B at J makes a dip, and the crossfade replaces it. `ClipLane.canHeal(after:)` is true when the gains of A and B are equal, the gain envelopes of A and B are equal at each file frame from the sounding start of A to the sounding end of B (0060), and A has no fade-out and B no fade-in: there a crossfade of any length changes no sample, because it reads only frames of that range. `ClipLane.newCrossfadeChangesNoSample(after:)` adds that the join has no crossfade before the call. Where it is true, `setCrossfades` with L > 0 throws `.crossfadeAtEqualGains`, `crossfadeLimit` is 0, and the file version does not change. Command-F skips a join where `canHeal(after:)` is true, also when it has a kept crossfade. At each other seamless join, Command-F makes the crossfade of a butt join. A kept crossfade at equal gains can change its length or go to 0. A gain edit that later makes the two gains equal keeps the crossfade, because only a broken join drops a crossfade (**Drop at a broken join**).
- **CLI.** `dirigible clip crossfade <package> --clip <A> --length <frames>` calls `setCrossfades`; `--length 0` removes it. The parser refuses an odd or negative length with exit 64. `dirigible info` adds `crossfade in L` or `crossfade out L` to a clip line, and `info --json` gives `crossfadeIn` and `crossfadeOut` only when not 0. `crossfadeLimit` does not change a session, so it has no command.
- **Errors.** The CLI gives each new case its own text. The window does not make an edit that throws one of the four cases, so the app shows the `errorSessionOther` text for each. `.crossfadeAtEqualGains` is a fifth case. Its CLI and MCP text says that the two clips play one piece of audio at one gain at each frame, the envelope included, with no fade at the join, so a crossfade there changes nothing. Only the reader of a file of version 8 or lower throws `.crossfadeAtSeamlessJoin`. Its text says that the file is from an older version that cannot hold that crossfade, and names the two attributes to remove. The window makes no edit that throws `.crossfadeAtEqualGains`, so the app shows the `errorSessionOther` text.
- **Render.** `MixReader`, the one mix of the Player and the export, finds a clip by its sounding range and renders the sounding clip. A render from J + 1 includes the end of A. The media check of an export reads the files of the sounding clips too.
- **11c-2: Command-F.** A range with in < J < out on a selected track makes a crossfade of L = 2 x min(J - in, out - J), centered on J, clamped to `crossfadeLimit`. A join also clamps to the room that the join before it leaves in the clip between them: the first join at a short clip takes the room, so the two new halves never pass the clip length. A range that holds A and B whole (Select All) gives 20 ms (960 frames at 48 kHz), rounded to even. A range that only touches J keeps the 0035 rule (no fade at a butt join). A limit of 0 skips that join, and no change gives a beep. One Command-F sets the crossfades first, then the fades of 0035 fitted to the room, in one undo entry.
- **11c-2: handles.** A crossfade shows two 8 x 8 pt handles, at J - L/2 in A and at J + L/2 in B, kept inside each box. A drag is relative to the press: a drag away from J adds 2 x its frames, and a drag toward J takes them off, clamped to 0 and to `crossfadeLimit` at the press; a drag to J removes it. A double-click on a handle removes it. The readout shows "Crossfade N ms". A join with no crossfade has no handle, so no drag makes a new crossfade. The drag is a new path of the 0020 budget, measured by its own bench.
- **11c-2: draw.** The waveform of each clip takes its own half of the curve inside its own box, so a 0 dBFS column at J is 3 dB under the top. The fade line of each clip continues over its handle into the neighbor box, in its own pass after the waveforms, in boxes of 12 pt or more, so the two lines cross at J as an X. That pass and `changedFrames` use the sounding range. The gain line and the gain band stop at the crossfade half, and a trim stops at half of the crossfade at the other edge.

## Consequences

- A build that reads version 3 or lower refuses a session with a crossfade. A session with no crossfade keeps its version 1, 2, or 3 text.
- A clip edit can remove a crossfade with no message. Undo restores it.
- A curve that follows from the join kind needs a version bump and no attribute. An asymmetric crossfade, a moved pivot, or a curve that the user chooses needs a new attribute and a version bump.
- A build that reads version 8 or lower refuses a session with a crossfade at a seamless join as a newer version. A session with no such crossfade keeps the version of the rule before.
- A crossfade at a seamless join can stay after a gain edit makes the two gains equal. It changes no sample, and the file stays version 9. Its handle drag can change its length, because the drag starts from the session at the press, which holds the crossfade (`crossfadeDrag`); a double-click or L = 0 removes it. A heal at that join drops it.

## Alternatives considered

- A crossfade stored on one clip: rejected, every edit and `changedFrames` would need a neighbor rule.
- A crossfade inside `Fades`: rejected, the fade handle and the inspector rebuild `Fades` and would drop it.
- An odd L with floor(L/2) before J: rejected, every UI path gives an even L, and an odd one needs a split rule.
- An overlap or a ripple edit when the handles are short (Final Cut Pro): rejected, 0035 forbids an overlap of clips.
- An asymmetric crossfade, or a pivot moved with Command-drag (Hindenburg): rejected, the crossfade stays centered on J (0035).
- A shorter crossfade in place of the drop when a split falls inside a reach: rejected, a drop keeps one rule for every broken join.
- A repair of only the changed clips, with no neighbors, or a local repair after an edit that removes clips away from its window: rejected, it leaves an orphan on an untouched neighbor, and every later save fails.
- A handle at every join that makes a crossfade by drag: rejected, it covers the trim zones at each speech edit.
- A shaded band or a waveform of the handle audio in the draw: rejected, a band costs a Swift loop per pixel.
- A curve stored in `Crossfades`, which `ClipLane` sets from the join kind (run `2026-10-17-agent-pack`, L2-S0 candidate A): rejected. It is a second copy of the join kind. Each of the eight places that build a `Crossfades` value must carry or derive it, also on clips outside a lane (the excerpt resample, the trims), and a length of 0 needs one fixed curve so that each `== .zero` check stays true.
- A curve per edge in `Fades`, which only the sounding clip of the lane sets: rejected. The public `Session.place(_:on:)` and `Session.setFades` take a caller's `Clip` or `Fades`, so a sounding value with the linear curve can go into a session that the file cannot save.
- A curve attribute in `session.xml` that the user chooses: rejected for now. At a seamless join equal power is always wrong, and at a butt join equal gain dips for two different signals, so the choice gives no correct new state.
