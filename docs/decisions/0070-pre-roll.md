# 0070. Pre-roll: playback during a pre-roll take, an anchor at the heard punch point, a floor at the playhead

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-10-28
- Amends: 0039 (playback during a take), 0044 (the playback line), 0004 (**Timing**: the take anchor and the floor), 0013 (**Start**: the Player start before the save in a pre-roll take), 0016 (Record, the Player during a take, the output latency), 0052 (the output latency line), 0021 (an aligned placement has the floor T; `.beforeTimeline` goes), 0047 (the prune moves the capture files of a pruned segment), 0048 (the `record` tool takes `pre_roll`), 0061 (the take time of a pre-roll take file), 0003 (the take anchor line and the pre-roll mark of `<recording>`), 0024 (the `session.xml` version of a pre-roll `<recording>` and anchor version 2).

## Context

F08 asks to record at the playhead after a pre-roll of the other tracks, with a length in Settings (Release 6 item 1). 0039 and 0044 say that no playback runs during a take and leave the choice to the pre-roll run. 0004 sets the take start host time at the save of the press. With a pre-roll, the user speaks against what they hear, so the clip must line up with the heard frame, and the output latency counts.

Hindenburg plays from before the in point and punches in at the in point [V-prerec]. Logic Pro Quick Punch-In records from the playback start and puts the region at the punch point; with input monitoring, the armed track plays its old audio before the punch [LPU]. The sources are in `docs/research/preroll-varispeed.md`.

Probes (2026-10-13, macOS 15.7.9, x86_64, Swift 6.2.4; run `2026-10-13-preroll-varispeed`, `probes/output-anchor*.txt`):

- PA1. `AVAudioPlayerNode.play(at:)` starts frame 0 at the chosen host time within 0.034 ms of the `lastRenderTime` mapping (three runs, spread 0.04 ms).
- PA2. `engine.start()` takes 267 to 450 ms on the built-in speakers; on a virtual device, 1.8 ms.
- PA3. `outputNode.presentationLatency` is 0.40 ms on the built-in speakers: the device latency (19 frames) only. The stream latency (284 frames) and the safety offset (77 frames) make 7.92 ms.
- PA4. Slice S1 step 0 and its diag, run `2026-10-28-pre-roll` (2026-11-03, MacBookPro15,2, macOS 15, 48 kHz, IO buffers of 512 frames; `probes/s1-step0/`, `probes/diag-roundtrip/`, `probes/diag-summary.md`). A click that `play(at:)` plays at a logged host time, recorded in a Command-R take of the built-in microphone and mapped with the formula of `Capture.alignedStart`, comes later than each HAL term by more than 2 ms. On the built-in speakers the median residual is +70.45 ms for device + stream + safety offset (7.92 ms), +72.05 ms for device + stream, and +77.97 ms for `presentationLatency`, with a spread of 0.23 ms over 40 clicks. On a Loopback virtual device (all terms 0) it is +95.69 ms. A raw-HAL round trip in one process puts the excess after the HAL `outOutputTime`: AVAudioEngine adds 0 ms (frame 0 renders at the asked host time), and the capture adds 0 ms (the sidecar stamps equal the raw `inInputTime`). The excess has three parts. One part is one output IO buffer: output buffers of 128, 512, and 2048 frames give round trips of 104.8, 118.2, and 148.8 ms on the built-in output, Loopback gives 85.04 ms + 1.0005 buffer, and the input buffer has no effect. One part changes by 0 to 18 ms at each output IO start. The last part, 47 to 65 ms on the built-in output and 85 ms on Loopback after one buffer, is in no HAL property (the stream latencies, the zero time stamp period, the transport type, the clock domain), and its side, output or input, is not known (item 1498).
- PA5. Other editors (`docs/research/recording-offset.md`): Reaper uses the latency that the driver reports and has manual input and output offsets; Logic Pro has a manual Recording Delay "to compensate for any delays that are caused by the audio driver"; Audacity 3.7.9 uses only a manual offset, default -130 ms. A calibration click needs a loopback path, which a laptop does not have.

## Decision

- **Command.** Record > Record with Pre-roll (key P) starts a take at the playhead T after a pre-roll. Command-R does not change: no playback during its take.
- **Length.** An app preference, 1 to 10 whole seconds, default 3. The pre-roll starts at S = max(0, T - length).
- **Start order.** The checks, the take mix through an awaited `update` with a new revision, the Player play command with a take start (the engine start and `play(at:)`), H_T, the save of `<recording>`, the IOProcs (`begin`), then the app taps. `prepareTake` takes the anchor as a closure that runs after the checks and before the save; a Command-R take passes none and keeps the save time. A check refusal starts no play. A save fault after the Player start stops the Player and changes nothing. A take play that stops at its start (its first refill) also stops the Player and changes nothing, and the start throws `preRollStopped` with that stop. No poll reads the take play, so its stop writes a journal line at the take end. The Player start writes a begin line and a line with the lead and H_T in the journal (0059).
- **Lead.** The Player is the one owner of the lead. `Command.play` carries T - S. `apply` reads the host time R after the engine start, the feed build, and the first refill, right before `play(at:)`, and takes the start host time as max(R + 100 ms, R + 0.5 s - (T - S) / the session rate), so H_T is at least 0.5 s after R and `play(at:)` never gets a past host time. When T - S is shorter than the lead, `play(at:)` waits. `Reading` gives the real start, and the Studio derives H_T from it. At playhead 0, P takes the same rule: T is heard 0.5 s after the engine start returns, with no playback before T.
- **Late start.** Only a pre-roll take checks it: an in-memory value of the take (`StartedTake.lateStartBase`, H_T), not a `Recording` field. The limit of each device writer is H_T + 3 IO buffers of its device, the length of its first input record. A track whose first frame, less its input latency, comes after the limit is in `TakeResult.lateStarts: Set<TrackID>`. It is a set of its own, not a `Gap` case, so it does not hide a later `writeFailed` or `deviceLost`. The take banner and the `dirigible record` warnings name each track in it.
- **Take mix.** The Player plays, at 1x, the session with the clips of the armed tracks cleared from T (`Session.clear`, no edit, no undo entry). The other heard tracks play whole, through the take. The heard set is the one at the press. Mute, solo, the fader, and edits stay off during a take (0039).
- **One start path.** `Player.apply` starts every play. `Command.play` has an optional take start (T - S, see **Lead**); with it, the node starts through a start-time seam (`play(at:)` on the device; in the paced output of 0016, amended 2026-10-30, `play()` with the paced base at the instant of the start host time, so the pull owes no frame before the start). The paced base is an instant of the continuous clock of the paced pull, and the start host time is a value of the host clock. The Player reads the two clocks together and sets the paced base to the continuous instant + (the start host time - the host time that it read), so each time that the Player compares comes from one clock. The take play so has a feed number, and the take stop ends it with `endFeed(stop: true)`. `Reading` gives the real start host time and the output term of **Anchor**; it stores no heard start, and the Studio derives none: `takeStartHostNanos(realStart:term:from:to:rate:)` adds `term.nanos(sessionRate:)` to the real start. A take feed keeps its base (the start host time and the start frame) across a rebuild. The host time of a frame is the base host time + the scheduled node frames / the session rate. A dry refill means that this host time already passed, so an underrun restart in a take feed uses `play(at:)` at now + 100 ms, and the anchor moves to the base frame + (the restart host time - the base host time) x the session rate. The frames whose time passed are skipped, and the heard-to-host map of the take does not change. The restart checks the picked host time after its refill loop, right before `play(at:)`; when it is late, the restart picks again from now, at most 3 times, then plays at once. A failed schedule in the restart also plays at once, as a plain refill does. A feed with no start host time, and a take feed of the paced or the offline output, keep the restart at once; in tests, a start seam on the offline output stands in for the device. In the paced output, a tick that owes more than 1 s of frames moves the paced base forward, in a take feed as in a plain play: the paced output plays to no device, and H_T and the live head use the host clock. `node.play` is called only in `apply` and in the restart path.
- **One Player session.** From the take play start to the take end, every session that the Studio sends to the Player is the take mix of that session, so an edit during the take (a take marker, a chapter), or a session sent before the save, keeps the cut at T. One play field holds the armed tracks and T for that time.
- **Anchor.** The heard host time of S is the `play(at:)` host time plus the output term. The output term is one value for the anchor and the playhead: the device latency + the stream latency + the safety offset + the IO buffer frame size (`kAudioDevicePropertyBufferFrameSize`, global scope) of the device of the engine output unit (`kAudioOutputUnitProperty_CurrentDevice`), in device frames at the nominal rate of that device, converted to nanoseconds once (PA3, PA4), read at each play start after the engine start. A buffer size change during a take does not move H_T. The two manual rendering outputs, offline and paced, have no output device: they keep the latency that the Player gets at its creation (`offlineLatency`). The input latency uses the same HAL reader, with its three terms and no buffer. The term is one value of one type: a device case (the four parts, the device rate, and the UID of the device) or a manual case (session frames). The playhead, the journal line, and H_T read that value, and the Studio stores no copy of it. A failed read of the device, of a part, of the buffer frame size, or of the UID, or a nominal rate of 0 or less, gives no term. The Player stores the term of a play start as one result, the term or its fault, and sets it to a fault at the start of each play start, so a failed engine start or a failed graph build leaves a fault, never the term of the last play. A plain play then uses `presentationLatency` for the playhead and writes the fault in the journal. A pre-roll take then stops the Player, keeps the playhead at T, and refuses with `outputTermUnavailable` before the save, so no `<recording>` is written. The input path keeps its rule: a failed stream latency reads 0. A seam of the Player and of the capture environment gives a fake term, so the Player and Studio tests see a term that is not 0. H_T = the heard host time of S + (T - S) / the session rate. `<recording>` holds T and H_T before the first IOProc starts. The journal line of the take start has the four parts, the device rate, the UID of the output device, the real start host time, and H_T.
- **Known limit.** The output term is the delay that the HAL reports and one IO buffer, not the full delay (PA4). On MacBookPro15,2 a pre-roll take lands 47 to 65 ms late on the built-in output and about 85 ms late on Loopback, and the late part changes by up to 18 ms between play starts. The Player position runs ahead of the sound by the same amount, and a take marker in the pre-roll goes that much late. A recording offset that the user sets (item 1500) and the side of the fixed part (item 1498) are later items.
- **Format.** A `<recording>` of a pre-roll take has `preRoll="true"`. `session.xml` takes the next version only while that `<recording>` is in it (0024 P6). That version is the next free number on main at the land, and the writer version (`sessionVersion`) is that number. The reader refuses `preRoll` in a file of a lower version. The anchor `Capture/take<n>.anchor` of a pre-roll take is version 2 (the same record; the version digit is the one pre-roll mark); a plain take keeps version 1. An older build so refuses the package with the newer-format message, or gives a per-segment newer fault for a placement, and never finalizes a pre-roll take at S, where its clip would cover the old armed audio from S to T. The floor needs no format change.
- **Floor.** The floor is a property of `.aligned`: `SegmentFinalize` takes it from `recording.playheadFrame`, in a take finalize (stop, recovery, retry, `dirigible take finalize`) and in a placement of an unplaced segment at its recorded time (0021) alike; `.fixed` (Place at Playhead) keeps 0. start = max(floor, anchor), offset = start - anchor. A segment with no frame after the floor becomes a pool file with no clip: its WAV is in `Clips/`, its CAF stays in `Capture/`. It is not an unplaced segment and does not block a ripple, and Remove Unused Files lists it. `.beforeTimeline` goes. `Studio.place` returns `ClipID?`: nil for a pool file with no clip.
- **Stop.** Stop, Space, and Command-Period end the take at the host time of the stop command: the writers end first, then the Player stops. Before T, the take ends with no clip, and the playhead goes back to T, so a new take records at the same T. A save fault also leaves the playhead at T.
- **During the take.** The live take draws from T. A take marker in the pre-roll goes at the heard play frame (the live head), clamped at 0. An output change stops the pre-roll sound; the take goes on and stays aligned, with no message. An agent transport call throws `.busy`, as in every take (0046).
- **Window.** A red line at T on the lanes and the ruler, drawn once, and the status "Pre-roll" until T.
- **Timed take (0048, 0011).** `dirigible record --pre-roll <seconds>` (0 to 10, default 0) and the `record` tool argument `pre_roll` call one rule. The take length counts from T: the writers end at H_T + the duration, and the timer stops the take after that time + the largest input latency of the armed devices + 250 ms. The consent sheet and the reply name the pre-roll. A window grant (0050) covers a request with a pre-roll.

## Consequences

- 0039, 0044: a pre-roll take plays the take mix; M and S stay off during a take.
- 0004 **Timing**: the take start host time of a pre-roll take is the heard host time of T; every aligned finalize and placement has the floor T. A plain take puts its frames before the press (at most the input latency) into the offset, so a take at T no longer cuts the tail of a clip that ends just before T.
- 0013 **Start**: a pre-roll take starts the Player between the checks and the save.
- 0016 and 0052: the Player position uses the output latency function of this record, not `presentationLatency`. The vari-speed ramp of 0052 **Position** (`Player.held`) lasts the latency in output frames after a speed change, so it gets longer: 892 frames (18.6 ms) at a 512-frame buffer on the built-in speakers of the test Mac, in place of the 19 frames (0.4 ms) of `presentationLatency`. The output path holds that many frames, so the ramp follows the term. The ramp is shorter than one playhead poll (33 ms). A take plays at 1x, so the ramp never moves H_T or a take. The unreported part is not in the ramp: at each speed the playhead runs ahead of the sound by that part (**Known limit**).
- 0061: take time 0 stays at T. File frame 0 of a pre-roll take file is at a negative take time, so a take-time import of a transcript of that file needs a negative `at`.
- A take file holds up to 10 s more audio. A cancelled pre-roll leaves a pool file with no clip.
- 0047: the prune of a pool file whose capture is a segment also moves that CAF and its sidecar to the Trash, after the durable write, in the order WAV, CAF, sidecar, when no other pool entry and no keeper refers to the capture. The Core scan gives the `CaptureID`s. DirigibleAudio, the one owner of the `Capture/` layout, gives the paths and sizes of the capture files through one `package` scan, `UnusedFiles(scanningWithCaptures:keptBy:keptFiles:in:)`. The app sheet, the app banners, the dry run of `file prune` and `file_prune`, and the move use that scan, so the list shows each file that the prune moves. The Core `UnusedFiles` carries the pruned `CaptureID`s.
- 0024: a package with a pre-roll take in progress or pending does not open in an older build. After the take ends, it opens again.
- 0016: the take play is a play command with a take start, and the Player chooses its start host time after the engine start; the underrun restart of a take feed skips the frames whose time passed and keeps its host map.
- `TakeResult` gets `lateStarts`, with an English and a Spanish banner text and a CLI warning line.

## Alternatives considered

- **`presentationLatency`, or the HAL terms with no buffer.** Rejected: PA4. Each is farther from the sound than the HAL terms and one buffer, and the round trip grows by one output buffer.
- **A calibration click for each device, or a fixed table for each transport type.** Not taken now. A click needs a loopback path that a laptop does not have, and the hidden part changes by up to 18 ms at each output start, so a stored value is wrong by up to that much. A table is a measured constant for each kind of device, which plan gate 1 refused for the code. Reverse of the term: either one, in a new decision.
- **A check of `lastRenderTime` after `play(at:)` that moves H_T.** Rejected: it rebuilds the render-time anchor, and PA1 shows `play(at:)` within 0.034 ms.
- **A pre-roll state with no take, and a take that starts about 1 s before T.** Rejected: a new Studio state and a timer, and a slow device start loses the first words after T.
- **A `Recording` flag that selects the floor for a pre-roll take only.** Rejected: the floor is correct for every take. The pre-roll mark of **Format** selects no rule; it only makes an older build refuse the take.
- **No version mark.** Rejected at plan gate 1: an older build would finalize a pre-roll take at S and cover the old armed audio from S to T.
- **A second Player start path for the take (`playForTake`).** Rejected at plan gate 1: it is outside the feed-number protocol, so a later command cannot end it by number.
- **Take time 0 at S.** Rejected: take time means the time from the record start, and Go to Take Time and the sync lines would change meaning.
- **Two calls in place of the anchor closure (`checkTakeStart`, `saveTakeStart`).** Rejected: two entries and a longer `startSteps` for the same order.
- **Only the other tracks play.** Rejected for now: the host does not hear the lead-in of the sentence that they record again. Reverse: clear the armed tracks from S.
- **Playback during every take.** Rejected: bleed on speakers at the start of a live interview.
