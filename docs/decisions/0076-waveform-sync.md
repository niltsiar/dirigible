# 0076. Sync a clip to a track by its audio waveform

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-28
- Accepted: 2026-11-18, after the plan gates (2 rounds) of run `2026-10-28-waveform-sync`.
- Amends: 0011 (`dirigible clip sync`), 0041 (the tool `clip_sync`), 0049 (a long job with progress and Cancel), 0051 (the agent manual names `clip_sync`), 0061 (a sync is an edit that can change a take sync state; no rule changes), 0063 (`clip_sync` reads files, so it is not a batch step: "sync" joins the list of work that a batch cannot hold). The drift correction of F74 amends this record, 0057 (an origin kind for the drift correction), and 0024 (the `session.xml` version of that kind).

## Context

F74 (R7, Release 6 item 5) aligns a clip to another track by its audio. The common case: a remote guest records a clean file on the guest computer, and the host imports it next to the call track of a Dirigible take. The two files have no shared host clock, so the take anchor of 0004 **Timing** cannot align them. Final Cut Pro has Clip > Synchronize Clips with "Use audio for synchronization" (`docs/research/final-cut-pro.md` section 7). Hindenburg PRO has no waveform sync that the research found. FCP makes a new synchronized clip and, with no sync point, leaves the clips at their starts with no error; PluralEyes corrects drift into a new file (`docs/research/waveform-sync.md`, findings 1 to 10).

Probes of the run (MacBookPro15,2, Intel i5-8259U): a vDSP FFT correlation of 1 h at 2 kHz takes 523 ms and finds the exact lag of a degraded copy. On real voices of the tester corpus, a 120 s host clip against its AAC 24 kbps copy gives the exact lag at 2 kHz, a normalized score of 0.993, and a peak to second peak ratio of 42.1 (plain) or 54.6 (GCC-PHAT); against the call track of another speaker it gives 0.018 and 0.99. A 1 kHz log-energy envelope finds the lag but has a broad peak (ratio 1.21), and its no-match score is 0.284, so it cannot decide a match.

## Decision

**Shared read.** `syncLag(of:to:in:package:span:progress:isCancelled:)` in `DirigibleAudio/DSP/` is the one owner of the sync read and match, as `speechRuns` is for the duck. `Studio.syncClip` and the closed-package work of the CLI and the tool both call it. It fails with no match (with the best score), a file fault, a cancel, or a read errno (`read(POSIXError)`, for example ENOMEM or EMFILE). `SyncResult` (the lag, the score, the spread) lives next to it and is the output of `syncLag` only: the head cut comes from the edit, not from the read.

**Streamed read.** The read streams the `MixReader` chunks of the reference span and decimates each chunk as it comes, with a FIR state that carries over between chunks, by the integer factor floor(rate / 2000). Only the 2 kHz reference and the clip excerpts stay in memory. The chunk loop is the one `scanMix` of `MixLevels.swift` (internal, with its sink); there is no second loop. For the fine step, the read gets again, for each excerpt, the clip excerpt and a reference window of the excerpt length plus 2 coarse frames on each side, both at the session rate. The reference FFT is computed once for the 3 excerpts, and the coarse work holds 4 float buffers of n in place (the reference spectrum, the excerpt work buffer, the window energies, the score) and two DFT setups, forward and inverse. At 2^24 points the setups hold about 100 MiB; an `FFTSetup` holds 579 MiB there. No memory for a setup refuses the sync with `read(POSIXError(.ENOMEM))`. No buffer holds session-rate audio longer than one chunk or one fine window.

**Match.** The coarse lag comes from an FFT cross-correlation (real DFTs of `vDSP_DFT_zrop_CreateSetup` with `vDSP_DFT_Execute`, `vDSP_zvmul` with the conjugate flag) on the 2 kHz signals. The score at each lag is the correlation over 4n (the scale of the packed real FFT pair) and over the root of the clip energy and the reference energy under the clip at that lag. That window energy is a sliding add and subtract in Double, given out as one Float per lag, so a quiet passage after a loud one does not cancel to 0. Only lags where the excerpt fully overlaps the reference are candidates. A lag whose window energy is under 1e-4 of the excerpt energy scores 0: on the probe voices the true lag has 0.969 and every lag of speech at least 0.489, and a -80 dBFS passage has 6.3e-6. So no score is over 1, and silence never wins. The peak is the largest score, not the largest raw correlation, so a loud unrelated passage of the reference does not win. The fine step refines the lag at the session rate in the window of the fine read. The lag converts to session frames by the factor, with no rate rounding.

**Match rule.** A match needs a score of 0.2 or more at the peak and a ratio of 3 or more between the peak and the largest value outside 50 ms of it. Else the result is no match, and the sync changes nothing. The coarse step is plain correlation.

**Excerpts.** The clip side is 3 excerpts of 20 s: one in each third of the clip, at the 20 s of that third with the most energy in the 50 ms window levels of the clip. A clip under 60 s gives one excerpt of the whole clip, so the longest excerpt is 60 s: e = ceil(60 x rate / factor) coarse samples, 120000 at 48 kHz and 120273 at 44.1 kHz (a coarse rate of 2004.5 Hz). Each excerpt carries its clip offset, so its lag is a clip lag. The thirds keep the excerpts apart. The lag is the median of the excerpt lags that match. The spread is the largest minus the smallest of those lags. With fewer than 2 matched excerpts the drift is not measured (never a spread of 0), and `SyncDrift` gives the cause: the clip is under 60 s, so it has one excerpt, or only one of the 3 excerpts matched, as a drift of 100 ppm on a codec copy can cause. The sync reports the spread and does not correct it.

**Search span.** The reference span is the clip range widened by `within` on each side (default 1800 s), cut to frame 0 and the session end. The FFT size n is the next power of 2 at or over the 2 kHz span length plus the longest excerpt (e samples), and n is at most 2^24 (256 MiB of buffers and about 100 MiB of DFT setups): the cut span is at most (2^24 - e) x factor frames (`Session.syncSpanCap`), 399773184 frames (8328.6 s) at 48 kHz and 366452746 frames (8309.6 s) at 44.1 kHz. The cap applies to the cut span, because the read holds only the cut span (`scanMix` renders the grid of the span and gives its sink only the span frames). The largest `within` is the largest w whose cut span is at most the cap: (cap - clip length) / 2 when neither side cuts, cap - clip length - the near side gap when one side cuts first, and no limit when the whole session fits. So a 1 h clip with no cut takes at most 2364 s at 48 kHz (2354 s at 44.1 kHz), and a 1 h clip at frame 0 takes at most 4728 s. An explicit `within` over the largest `within` throws `SessionError.syncSpanTooLong(maxWithin:)`; the default `within`, and every sync from the app, clamps to it (`Session.syncWithin`), which the default 1800 s needs only for a clip over 4728.6 s (48 kHz) with 1800 s free on each side; a clip whose own range is over the cap throws `.syncSpanTooLong(maxWithin: 0)`. The reference audio is the mix of the reference track with mute and solo ignored, downmixed to mono. The clip audio is the clip alone, with its gain, fades, and envelope, read through a session that holds only that clip on its track (`Session.syncSource`).

**File faults.** A clip file or a reference file in the span that is `.missing` or `.unreadable` refuses the sync with the file ID and the `MediaFault`; it never reads as no match. Before the stream, the sync checks only the files that it reads: `checkMedia` on `Session.syncSource` with `ExportSource(range: span, tracks: [clip track, reference track])`. So a muted reference track is checked, a fault at the span end refuses before any read, and a missing file of a clip that the sync does not read never blocks it. A `.short` file reads the frames that exist.

**Edit.** On a match, `Session.sync(_:by:)` moves the clip by the lag with the rules of `Session.nudge`: the clip keeps its ID, the clips under its new range on its track are cut, and a crossfade whose join the move breaks drops. A new start under frame 0 cuts the head of the clip to frame 0; a new end at or under frame 0 throws `.frameOutOfRange`. `Session.syncSpan` refuses a reference that is the track of the clip (`SessionError.syncSameTrack`) or that has no clip in the span (`SessionError.noReferenceAudio`). `Session.defaultSyncReference(for:)` gives the first other track with a `.app(bundleID:)` route that holds a clip in the default span, else the first other track that holds a clip there.

**Job.** `Studio.syncClip(_:to:within:agent:progress:)` runs `syncLag` through `runReadJob` on a new transport case `.syncing`: the read runs off the main actor, every other entry point throws `.busy`, the banner shows progress and Cancel, and a change saves `session.xml` and records one undo entry with the agent. It returns the `SyncResult` and the head cut of the edit. It throws `StudioError.syncNoMatch(best:)`, `.syncFile(FileID, MediaFault)`, `.syncRead(Int32)` (in the shape of `duckRead`), or `.syncCancelled`. No match is not a `SessionError` case, because `Session` never decides it.

**Surface (0011, 0041, 0049).** `dirigible clip sync <package> <clip> --to <track> [--within <seconds>] [--json]` and the tool `clip_sync` (`clip`, `to`, `within`). The text: `moved <clip> by +1.234 s (59232 frames), match 0.98`, plus `the lag changes by 46 ms across the clip` when the spread is over 20 ms (no cause named: a call app can also vary its delay), or, when the drift was not measured, `the drift was not measured: the clip is shorter than 60 s` or `the drift was not measured: only one part of the clip matched`, and `cut 6.000 s at the head` after a head cut, then `replaced audio of B, D` with each other clip on the track of the clip that the move cut or removed (the banner names them too, so no silent cut of another part reaches an export). No match exits 1; a bad argument exits 64, and so does a `within` over the cap, with a message that names the largest `within` for that clip in the spelling of its front end (`--within` or `within`), or says to leave the argument out when that largest `within` is under 1 s; a file fault exits 2 with the file and the fault, and a read errno exits 2 with the errno text. In the app, the tool runs as a long job through `Studio.syncClip`, and a moved clip with take audio adds the `Take sync:` line of 0061. The app has Tools > Sync Clip to Track... with Option-Command-G; its sheet has the reference popup, prefilled by `defaultSyncReference`, and no `within` field.

## Consequences

- The sync reads the clip excerpts and a cut reference span of at most the cap ((2^24 - e) x factor frames, 8328.6 s at 48 kHz) per call; the cost is the read, not the FFT. The bench `SyncLagBenchmark` asserts the time (at most 7.5 s, 2 times its first optimized time) and the peak footprint (at most 512 MiB) at the cap in an optimized build.
- A clip longer than about 2.3 h cannot sync with a useful `within`: the cap bounds the clip range plus the span.
- A clip whose lag changes is aligned at its median lag only. The result text gives the change, and the drift correction of F74 corrects a clock drift.
- A clip with take audio that the user syncs leaves its take sync state; 0061 reports it.

## Alternatives considered

- **One full-rate correlation of the whole clip.** Rejected: 1 h against 1 h at 48 kHz needs n = 2^28, about 16 times the 2 kHz work, and 1 GiB for one float buffer.
- **The reference span held at the session rate and decimated inside the match.** Rejected at plan gate 1: a 2 h span is 1.38 GB of mono float.
- **GCC-PHAT for the coarse step.** Not chosen: on the fixtures of the run it gives the same lags as plain correlation and costs the same, but it whitens over the whole reference, so a loud passage anywhere in the span lowers the true peak. A burst 23 dB louder gives a PHAT ratio of 2.38, under the rule of 3; plain keeps a ratio of 26 or more at 30 dB. A real call track against a codec-degraded copy passes the rule in plain correlation.
- **A 1 kHz log-energy envelope for the coarse step** (research default). Not chosen: on the probe voices its peak is too broad for the ratio rule, and a wrong track scores 0.284.
- **60 s excerpts.** Rejected: at 100 ppm a 60 s excerpt smears the lag by 6 ms, so the median peak falls to 0.205 and only one excerpt matches; 20 s excerpts give 0.411 and a spread of 78 ms.
- **A correlator type with a stored FFT setup and an audio-source protocol.** Rejected: one conformer and one size per call. The drift correction reuses the functions with several windows; a stored setup is a measured change if its bench needs it.
